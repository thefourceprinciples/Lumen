# The Living Framework Garden Protocol (`G00-T001` / `FR-013`)

## 1. Core Invariant & Governing Laws
> **Ideas are living seeds; silos are soil; attention is water; artifacts are fruit; cross-links are mycelium; the hearth is the return point.**

- **Primary Archive Law:** *"No harvest may claim more archive than it can see."* (Every census, summary, or status report must explicitly declare its visible context bounds and missing records.)
- **Anti-Coercion Law:** *"Do not resurrect old fire by force."* (Dormant threads are composted or re-watered organically when evidence and utility align.)
- **Hearth Return Phrase:** *"The hearth is the living center we return to."*

## 2. Seven-Stage Cultivation Lifecycle
Every concept, dataset, or software module in Lumen matures through seven auditable stages:
1. **Seed:** Raw intuition, question, or unverified hypothesis captured at intake.
2. **Soil:** Contextual silo, source corpus, and domain constraints ($K$) nourishing the seed.
3. **Roots:** Foundational definitions, primary sources (`P1–P4`), and falsification rules anchoring the idea.
4. **Trunk:** Core invariant thesis—the single controlling definition that survives perturbation.
5. **Branches:** Specialized sub-protocols, implementations, and domain extensions.
6. **Flowers:** Active testing, cross-silo synthesis, and peer/steward critique.
7. **Fruit:** Durable, usable, verified artifacts (code, specs, monographs, datasets) harvested into the ledger.

## 3. Watering Levels & Mycelial Links
- **Level-1 Watering:** Initial capture, schema tagging, and boundary placement.
- **Level-2 Watering:** Cross-referencing against existing Garden Beds and testing against CUC constraints.
- **Level-3 Watering:** Full reconciliation, adversarial falsification, and promotion into fruiting artifacts.
- **Mycelial Links:** Explicit cross-domain edges connecting distinct Garden Beds (`G00` Hearth & Governance, `G01` Coherence Foundations, `G02` AI Identity & Continuity, `G03` Chronovisor & Living Atlas, etc.) without collapsing their distinct domain rules.
