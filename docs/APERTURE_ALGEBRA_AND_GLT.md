## 1. Formal Definition of a Parametric Orbit Aperture Field (`POAF`)
A **POAF** is a spatially distributed, static or time-dependent boundary-control function whose geometry is generated from parameterized curves, dynamical trajectories, or indexed orbit families (e.g., Lissajous masks, phyllotactic point-orbit arrays, moiré multilayer apertures, fold-state apertures, and graphene registry fields) mapped into amplitude, phase, impedance, stiffness, or hopping modulation.

## 2. Five-Object Separation & Mathematical Guarantees
To prevent visual geometry from being conflated with physical eigenmodes or unverified quantum claims, POAF enforces strict separation across five objects:
1. **Generating Orbit** ($\gamma(t)$)
2. **Observational Projection** ($\Pi(\gamma)$)
3. **Aperture Field** ($\mathcal{A}(x, y, t)$)
4. **Physical Realization** (declared material, excitation, and boundary conditions)
5. **Measured Response** ($\mathcal{R}[\mathcal{A}]$ via declared measurement channels)

### Core Formal Propositions
- **Arc-Length Invariance:** Geometric support is invariant under regular reparameterization when the orbit-to-field map uses arc-length measure.
- **Projection Non-Injectivity:** Projected recurrence in 2D visual space does **not** imply full-state recurrence in higher-dimensional phase space when the observation map is non-injective.
- **Lipschitz Stability Transfer:** Bounded propagation operators transfer Lipschitz stability of aperture design parameters to bounded output variation.
